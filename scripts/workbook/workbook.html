About this workbook

It's a planning tool, not a diet. The guidance inside is general and practical, and there are no rules to follow perfectly. Skip any page that doesn't suit your week, and come back to it when it does.

Getting started

How to use this workbook

Each week follows the same simple rhythm: plan, shop, prep, review. Use as much or as little as suits you. Even planning a few dinners ahead can take some of the guesswork out of the week.

01

Plan

Sit down once a week with your diary. Mark the busy evenings first, then fill in the meal planner, starting with dinners. Pencil in one "cook once, eat twice" meal to cover a busy day.

02

Shop

Check the fridge, freezer and pantry pages before you write anything. Note what needs using up, then build the shopping list by aisle, straight from your plan.

03

Prep

Choose a prep session that fits your week: one longer slot or two short ones. Wash and chop, cook a grain or a pot of beans, and portion meals into labelled containers.

04

Review

At the end of the week, note what worked, what to swap and which meals to repeat. Carry leftovers and good ideas straight into next week's plan.

Reuse ingredients, waste less

  • Roast a large tray of vegetables: serve them as a side one night, then add them to a grain bowl, wrap or soup.
  • Cook a pot of grains or lentils once and build two or three meals around it.
  • Pick one bunch of herbs and one leafy green for the week and use them in several dishes.
  • Plan a use-it-up meal near the end of the week, such as a frittata, soup, fried rice or a tray bake.
  • Freeze portions you won't eat in the next couple of days, and note them on the freezer inventory.
  • Buy tins, jars and frozen staples you'll use across several meals, rather than one-off ingredients.

A one-page guide

Build a balanced plate

A quick visual check for planning meals. It isn't a rule for every plate: think about balance across the day and the week.

Vegetables & fruit About half

Mix colours and types across the week. Fresh, frozen and tinned all count; for tins, choose ones in water or juice, with no added sugar or salt where you can.

Protein About a quarter

Rotate between beans, lentils, chickpeas, tofu, eggs, fish, poultry and lean meat. Pulses are an easy, low-cost staple to keep in the cupboard.

Whole grains & starchy foods About a quarter

Choose wholegrain or higher-fibre versions where you can: oats, brown rice, wholewheat pasta, wholegrain bread, potatoes with their skins on.

Healthy fats A little

Cook with or dress food with small amounts of oils such as olive or rapeseed oil, and add nuts, seeds or avocado now and then.

Water Through the day

Make water your usual drink, and keep a glass or bottle within reach at meals.

Using the plate when you plan

  • Look at each dinner on your planner and ask: is there a vegetable, a protein, and a grain or starchy food?
  • If a meal is light on vegetables, add an easy side such as a salad, frozen peas or a tray of roasted vegetables.
  • Breakfasts, lunches and snacks can fill gaps: fruit with breakfast, a handful of nuts, a bean-based lunch.
General guidance for healthy adults. If you have a medical condition, are pregnant, or follow a clinical diet, follow your clinician's advice.

Shopping trips

Ways to stretch the budget

  • Check the pantry and freezer pages before writing your list, so you don't buy what you already have.
  • Plan meals that share ingredients across the week.
  • Compare the unit price on shelf labels, not just the pack price.
  • Frozen and tinned vegetables, pulses and fish keep well and help cut waste.

Month

Record what you batch-cook, how many portions it made and where they're stored. Write the same date on the container label.

Keeping leftovers safe

  • Cool leftovers quickly and refrigerate them within about 2 hours of cooking. Shallow containers help them cool faster.
  • Eat refrigerated leftovers within 2–3 days, or freeze them.
  • Reheat food until it is steaming hot all the way through.
  • Label and date every container before it goes in the fridge or freezer.

Follow the food-safety guidance in your country.
